Why the hour matters

Dubai is hot for most of the year. From June to September the afternoons often pass 40 °C, and by the sea the air is wet as well. On foot, the hour you go matters as much as the way you go.

Early in the morning the sun is low in the east, and the towers throw shadows hundreds of meters long across the promenades and the water. As it climbs, the shadows draw in. In June the noon sun stands almost straight overhead, and a tower’s shade is no wider than its foot: that is the hour to be indoors, in the mall, or under the road bridge. In the late afternoon the shadows come back, falling the other way.

So the side matters too. On the Marina Walk side the towers stand behind the promenade to the east, and shade it in the morning. On the JBR side they stand to the west, and shade it in the afternoon. The beach has no shade but the first hour’s, before the sun clears the towers of JBR, and the bridge to Bluewaters has none at all.
